\begin{abstract}
We enumerate labeled simple graphs on $n$ vertices by their clique number,
giving the triangle $d(n,k)$ of graphs with clique number exactly $k$.
The triangle is recorded in the OEIS as A395684; its bounded-multiplicity
extensions and cumulative distribution are A395691--A395695.
It provides an exact finite distribution of clique sizes and, under the
chosen clique-dominance convention $K>\omega(G)$, of the threshold
$\Kdom=\omega(G)+1$. We give verified values for $n\leq8$, exact
near-diagonal formulas, the weighting formula for labeled $p$-multigraphs,
and reproducible computational and formal verification material.
This revision retains the enumerative results and all independently
reproduced sequence terms. A dated corrigendum clarifies the precision of
the random-graph approximation, the expansion discussion, related OEIS
comparisons, an overlap expression, and the distinction between a chosen
dominance threshold and a necessary minimum for lossless decoding.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The Clique-Cluster (CC) transformation replaces each input vertex with a
coding clique and represents an edge of multiplicity $q$ by $q$
same-index edges between its coding cliques \cite{zarzuelo2025}.
For a simple input graph $G$, choosing a coding size $K>\omega(G)$
ensures that coding cliques are larger than transversal cliques.
The least integer satisfying this imposed dominance condition is
\[
  \Kdom(G)=\omega(G)+1.
\]
This is a prescribed coding rule, rather than a claim that no smaller
lossless encoding or different decoder is possible.

The natural enumerative question is how clique numbers are distributed
over all $2^{\binom n2}$ labeled graphs on $n$ vertices. The present triangle
answers that question exactly. Its unlabeled counterpart is OEIS
A263341, using graph complementation. The labeled triangle and its
extensions were accepted as A395684 and A395691--A395695 in May 2026
\cite{oeis}. Their definitions and counts are independent of the CC
interpretation and of random-graph asymptotics.

The resulting distributions are relevant to finite graph enumeration,
$K_{k+1}$-free graph counting, and analysis of the chosen coding rule.
Section~11 records the clarifications to the May 2026 edition.
These clarifications do not alter the finite enumerative contribution.

\section{Definitions}
Let $\Graph$ denote the simple graphs on the fixed labeled vertex set
$[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}$, with $n\geq1$.
\begin{definition}[Clique number]
For $G=([n],E)$, define
\[
 \omega(G)=\max\{|S|:S\subseteq[n],\ \binom S2\subseteq E\}.
\]
Here $\binom S2$ is the set of unordered pairs of distinct vertices
of $S$.
\end{definition}
\begin{definition}[Dominance threshold]
The clique-dominance threshold is $\tau(G)=\omega(G)+1$.
Thus $\tau(G)$ is the least integer $K$ satisfying $K>\omega(G)$.
For simple graphs we also write $\Kdom(G)=\tau(G)$.
\end{definition}
\begin{definition}[Exact distribution]
For $1\leq k\leq n$, define
\[
 d(n,k)=|\{G\in\Graph:\omega(G)=k\}|.
\]
Set $d(n,k)=0$ outside this range. These are the rows of A395684.
\end{definition}
\begin{definition}[Cumulative distribution]
Define
\[
 D(n,k)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}d(n,j),\qquad D(n,0)=0.
\]
Equivalently, $D(n,k)$ counts graphs with clique number at most $k$.
These are the rows of A395695.
\end{definition}

\section{Main Results}
\subsection{Structural properties}
\begin{theorem}[Boundary values]
For $n\geq1$, $d(n,1)=d(n,n)=1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A graph has clique number one exactly when it has no edges, so the
empty graph is the unique member of the first bucket. A graph on
$n$ vertices has clique number $n$ exactly when it is complete.
\end{proof}
\begin{theorem}[Row sum]
For $n\geq1$,
\[
 \sum_{k=1}^{n}d(n,k)=2^{\binom n2}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each of the $\binom n2$ possible edges is present or absent independently
in specifying a labeled graph. The clique-number buckets partition
this finite set.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Cumulative counts and inclusion--exclusion}
\begin{theorem}[Difference formula]
Let $c(n,k)$ count $K_{k+1}$-free labeled graphs. Then
\[
 D(n,k)=c(n,k),\qquad d(n,k)=c(n,k)-c(n,k-1),
\]
with $c(n,0)=0$ and $c(n,n)=2^{\binom n2}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A graph is $K_{k+1}$-free exactly when its clique number is at most $k$.
Subtracting graphs with clique number at most $k-1$ leaves those
with clique number exactly $k$.
\end{proof}
For completeness, ordinary inclusion--exclusion gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ie}
 c(n,k)=\sum_{\mathcal F\subseteq\binom{[n]}{k+1}}
 (-1)^{|\mathcal F|}
 2^{\binom n2-\left|\bigcup_{S\in\mathcal F}\binom S2\right|}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, the intersection of clique events indexed by $\mathcal F$
forces exactly the edges in the displayed union; every other edge is
free. The empty family contributes $2^{\binom n2}$.

\subsection{Near-diagonal formulas}
\begin{theorem}[Near-diagonal closed forms]
For $n\geq2$,
\begin{align*}
 d(n,n-1)&=n2^{n-1}-\binom n2-n,\\
 c(n,n-2)&=2^{\binom n2}-n2^{n-1}+\binom n2+n-1.
\end{align*}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For each vertex $v_i$, let $A_i$ be the event that $[n]\setminus\{v_i\}$
is a clique. Then $|A_i|=2^{n-1}$, and $|A_i\cap A_j|=2$ for
$i\neq j$: only the edge $\{v_i,v_j\}$ remains free. An intersection
of three or more distinct events forces the complete graph and has
size one. Thus
\[
 \left|\bigcup_i A_i\right|
 =n2^{n-1}-2\binom n2+
 \sum_{j=3}^{n}(-1)^{j+1}\binom nj
 =n2^{n-1}-\binom n2-n+1.
\]
Removing the complete graph proves the first formula. Subtracting
$d(n,n-1)+d(n,n)$ from the total gives the second. The empty higher
intersection sum at $n=2$ gives the same identities.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}[Overlap bookkeeping]\label{rem:overlap}
General intersections depend on the entire overlap pattern.
For two $(k+1)$-subsets intersecting in $t$ vertices, the forced-edge
count is
\[
 2\binom{k+1}{2}-\binom t2.
\]
More generally it is the cardinality of the edge union in
\eqref{eq:ie}, or equivalently
\[
 \sum_{\varnothing\neq I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,r\}}
 (-1)^{|I|+1}\binom{\left|\bigcap_{i\in I}S_i\right|}{2}.
\]
This corrects the informal overlap expression in the original
Remark~3.1; the tabulations and near-diagonal proof do not use that
expression.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Extension to bounded-multiplicity graphs}
\begin{definition}[$p$-multigraph]
A labeled $p$-multigraph assigns a multiplicity
$\mu:\binom{[n]}2\to\{0,1,\ldots,p\}$. Its clique number is that of
the underlying simple graph with edge set $\{e:\mu(e)>0\}$.
\end{definition}
\begin{theorem}[Weighted distribution]
For $p\geq1$, the number $d_p(n,k)$ of these multigraphs with clique
number $k$ is
\[
 d_p(n,k)=\sum_{\substack{G\in\Graph\\\omega(G)=k}}p^{e(G)}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each present edge of a fixed support graph $G$ has $p$ possible
positive multiplicities. Nonedges have multiplicity zero. Hence $G$
has exactly $p^{e(G)}$ preimages. Summing over supports proves the
formula.
\end{proof}
\begin{corollary}[Multigraph row sum]
$\sum_{k=1}^{n}d_p(n,k)=(p+1)^{\binom n2}$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Each unordered vertex pair has $p+1$ multiplicity choices, including
zero. Clique-number buckets partition those assignments.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Dominance-threshold distribution}
For simple graphs, the number receiving $\Kdom=k+1$ under the
specified dominance rule is exactly $d(n,k)$: it is the same table
with shifted column labels. For a multigraph with actual maximum
multiplicity $P$, the safe rule in the cited construction is
$K=\max(P,\omega(G))+1$; clique number alone does not specify its
entire parameter choice.

\subsection{Verification scope}
The supplied clique oracle was checked against explicit vertex-subset
testing for all 33,867 graphs with $1\leq n\leq6$. Separate exhaustive
runs checked all 2,097,152 seven-vertex graphs and all eight-vertex
graphs. The resulting $(\omega,e)$ histograms reproduce all five
weighted distributions and their exact totals. Cumulative rows are
checked by termwise prefix sums.
The later published rows, through $n=12$ for $p=1$ and $n=11$ for
$p=2,\ldots,5$, additionally pass endpoint, total and near-diagonal
checks. Their larger interior entries were not exhaustively recomputed
in the October verification. These limits are recorded explicitly
with the reproducibility material.

valid chromatic-number comparison.

\section{Algorithmic Implementation}
The independent verification records, for each edge count $e$, the
number $H_n(k,e)$ of graphs with clique number $k$. It then evaluates
\[
 d_p(n,k)=\sum_{e=0}^{\binom n2}H_n(k,e)p^e.
\]
One oracle tests candidate vertex subsets directly for all required
edges. A second uses the induced-subgraph recurrence
\[
 \omega(G[S])=\max\bigl(\omega(G[S\setminus\{v\}]),\,
 1+\omega(G[(S\setminus\{v\})\cap N(v)])\bigr).
\]
This follows by separating cliques that omit $v$ from those that
contain it. The eight-vertex run enumerates all seven-vertex graphs
and all possible neighborhoods of the last vertex.
The repository includes portable scripts, saved expected terms,
per-term comparisons, result files and exact-arithmetic checks.
Assertions compare clique buckets themselves; totals are additional
checks rather than substitutes for classification verification.

\section{Applications and Interpretation}
The primary application is the exact distribution of a standard
graph invariant across finite labeled graphs. The cumulative values
give counts of $K_{k+1}$-free graphs, and the weighted extensions
give the analogous finite census with bounded edge multiplicities.
These are concrete enumerative objects independent of any encoding.

For the chosen clique-dominance coding rule, $d(n,k)$ also gives the
number of simple graphs assigned each coding size $k+1$. It can
therefore describe that convention's allocation costs. It does not
claim optimality over all graph encodings or decoding rules.
Connections with extremal and Ramsey questions require their own
additional arguments; a finite clique-number distribution alone
does not determine a Fraisse limit or establish performance on an
empirical network population.

\section{Asymptotic Analysis}
\begin{proposition}[Clique number of a uniform random graph]
For $G\sim G(n,1/2)$,
\[
 \Pr(\omega(G)=k)=\frac{d(n,k)}{2^{\binom n2}}.
\]
The first-order approximation is
\[
 \frac{\omega(G)}{2\log_2n}\ \xrightarrow{\Pr}\ 1.
\]
More precisely,
\[
 \omega(G)=2\log_2n-2\log_2\log_2n+O(1)
 \quad\text{with high probability}.
\]
There is an integer sequence $k_n=2\log_2n-2\log_2\log_2n+O(1)$
such that $\Pr(\omega(G)\in\{k_n,k_n+1\})\to1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every labeled graph is equiprobable, proving the exact identity.
The asymptotic statements are the classical clique-number results
of Bollobas and Erdos, including the finer concentration statement
\cite{bollobaserdos1976,bollobas2001}.
\end{proof}
\begin{proposition}[Expansion under the dominance rule]
For the chosen simple-graph rule $\Kdom=\omega(G)+1$,
\[
 \mathbb E[\Kdom]=2\log_2n-2\log_2\log_2n+O(1).
\]
Its expected output vertex count is
\[
 n\,[2\log_2n-2\log_2\log_2n+O(1)].
\]
Thus the expected vertex count is $\Theta(n\log n)$, a logarithmic expansion
factor relative to the input's $n$ vertices.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use the classical expectation estimate for the clique number
\cite{bollobaserdos1976,bollobas2001} and the identities
$\Kdom=\omega+1$ and $|V(f_{CC}(G))|=n\Kdom$ for this rule.
\end{proof}
This is a statement about the specified uniform model and coding
convention. It does not by itself establish runtime efficiency,
optimal encoding size, or typical behavior of real-world graphs.

\section{Conclusion}
The triangles $d(n,k)$, $d_p(n,k)$ and $D(n,k)$ give exact finite
counts by clique number, bounded multiplicity and cumulative clique
bound. Their original tabulated values remain unchanged and are
supported by additional independent termwise reproduction.
The clique-dominance threshold $k+1$ remains a well-defined
interpretation under the chosen coding convention. Separating that
convention from necessary minimum encoding size, and separating
first-order asymptotics from precise concentration, makes the scope
of the result explicit without changing its enumerative content.

\section{Corrigendum to the May 2026 Edition}\label{sec:corrigendum}
This section records clarifications identified in an author-initiated
review, supported by additional independent verification. The
finite enumeration is the paper's main result. Its definitions,
retained tables, near-diagonal closed forms and multigraph weighting
formula are unchanged. None of the corrections below changes any
independently reproduced term of A395684 or A395691--A395695.

\subsection{Precision of the original Proposition 9.1}
The expression $2\log_2n$ is a valid first-order approximation to
the clique number of $G(n,1/2)$. In the original Proposition~9.1,
that approximation was used more precisely than justified by saying
the probability concentrates on its floor or ceiling. The actual
concentration location includes the unbounded second-order term
$-2\log_2\log_2n$, as now stated in Proposition~9.1.
The exact identity $\Pr(\omega=k)=d(n,k)/2^{\binom n2}$ is unchanged.
The approximation was not used to compute the finite tables.

\subsection{Expansion caveat and finite-data caption}
For the specified dominance rule, the first-order output estimate
$n(2\log_2n+1)$ describes the leading scale. The more precise
expectation is given in Proposition~9.2. The phrase ``only
marginally larger'' is replaced by the explicit description
``a logarithmic expansion factor.'' Statements about a uniform
random-graph model do not establish performance on empirical
network distributions. Figure~1 now describes the actual finite
rows rather than using the first-order asymptotic expression as
their concentration center.

\subsection{Related sequence identifications}
A058843 counts properly colored labeled graphs, identifying
permutations of color names; it does not count graphs by their
chromatic number. Its $n=4,k=2$ entry is $80$. The original
chromatic-number comparison using $31$ is replaced by the correct
description in Section~5. The corresponding unlabeled
$n=4,k=2$ entry of A263341 is $6$, rather than the neighboring
entry $3$. These were comparison descriptions, not inputs to the
enumeration algorithms. The labeled value $d(4,2)=40$ is unchanged.

\subsection{Dominance convention and lossless recovery}
The value $k+1$ is the least integer satisfying the imposed
condition $K>k$. It should not be read as a necessary minimum for
every lossless decoder. Indeed, the cited same-index wiring admits
the following elementary recovery method when $K>P$, with $P$
the maximum multiplicity.

Every vertex of a coding clique has its $K-1$ internal neighbors.
The unused index $K$ has no external neighbors, so the minimum
degree of the image is exactly $K-1$. Each vertex of this degree
has closed neighborhood equal to its coding clique. Taking the
distinct such neighborhoods recovers the entire cluster partition;
counting the edges between each pair recovers its multiplicity.
Thus $K=P+1$ suffices for this decoder without a bound on the
input clique number. For simple graphs, $K=2$ suffices.

The triangle therefore measures clique number and the assigned
threshold under the dominance convention, not an optimal cost
over all decoding methods. This corrects the necessity/minimum
interpretation while preserving the enumeration and the
sufficient largest-clique identification argument. It also avoids
the earlier tension between a chosen coding rule and general
recoverability.

\subsection{Overlap expression and formal status}
Remark~3.1 in the original edition contained an incorrect overlap
expression. The correct two-set subtraction is $\binom t2$ when
the vertex intersection has size $t$; Remark~\ref{rem:overlap} now
gives the edge-union formulation. That expression was not used
to generate the retained tables or prove the near-diagonal formulas.
The updated formalization material states its exact proved scope,
separately from cited asymptotics and finite computational checks.
Historical sources and the original editions remain available
in the repository archive.

\subsection{Reproducibility and preservation of the result}
The supplementary verification compares individual entries, not
only row totals. It independently reproduces all six distributions
through eight vertices, including weighted and cumulative values.
The corrected comparisons, asymptotic precision, overlap remark
and decoding interpretation do not enter those computations.
The accepted finite enumerative contribution and its use as a
labeled-graph clique-number census are therefore preserved.
